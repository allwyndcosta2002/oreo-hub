Lock in with the transporter

The Hunter Valley to Waitara is about 135 km, roughly 1 hr 45 min direct. Oreo will only have his first (C3) vaccine, so the van is the biggest parvo risk of the whole move: other dogs, shared crates and roadside toilet stops. Get these answers in writing before you pay.

Delivery day at home

  1. Clear the whole delivery window. ETAs drift on shared runs. Someone stays home with their phone on loud.
  2. Pen ready before he leaves the breeder: grass tray, bed, water bowl, and his first meal of the breeder's food measured out.
  3. Meet the van downstairs. Take the hand-over at the kerb or in the car park, then carry him (in the transport crate or your arms) through the lift to your flat. No common-area grass; other building dogs use it.
  4. Two-minute arrival check before you sign (below). Photograph Oreo and his crate, and write any concerns on the delivery paperwork.
  5. Straight to the grass tray, quiet praise when he goes.
  6. Small drink now, small meal later. Offer water straight away. After 30–60 minutes, if he's settled, give about a quarter of a normal meal. A long trip can drop a small puppy's blood sugar.
  7. Then quiet: let him explore the pen and sleep. No visitors. Message the breeder that he's arrived.
Arrival check: bright and responsive · gums pink and moist (press one; colour returns in about 2 seconds) · quiet, easy breathing · no vomit, diarrhoea or blood in the crate · skin at the scruff springs back when gently lifted · walks steadily.
Call a vet the same day if he's flat or wobbly, his gums are pale, dry or sticky, he vomits more than once, has diarrhoea, or won't eat by evening. If he seems weak, rub a little honey on his gums on the way: small puppies can drop their blood sugar.

First 72 hours: night survival

  • Crate beside your bed for the first week or two. Your fingers at the door and your breathing nearby do more than any gadget.
  • Scent and warmth: the breeder's litter blanket, a heartbeat-style snuggle toy, and a warm (not hot) wrapped water bottle. Skip microwaved wheat bags: they hot-spot and burn.
  • Don't "cry it out" at 8 weeks. He has just lost his mother and littermates. Answer cries with a calm, low voice and a hand at the crate. Settling him isn't spoiling him; panic teaches the crate is scary.
  • One silent night toilet trip (around 2–3 am) for roughly the first two weeks: carry him to the grass tray, no talking or play, straight back to bed.
  • No welcome party. Keep visitors away for 3 days. Sleep, eat, toilet, short play is the whole job.
  • Start alone-training on Day 1 with 30-second gaps: see the Mid-January protocol on the Routine tab.
  • Own-vet health check within 72 hours (check the deadline in your breeder's health guarantee).
Go to a vet now if: he hasn't eaten for 12 hours, is limp or wobbly (small puppies can drop their blood sugar: rub a little honey on his gums on the way), vomits repeatedly, has diarrhoea with blood, or breathes noisily or with effort. Emergency numbers are on the Vet tab.

4th-floor balcony: the one hazard that can't have a second chance

An 8-week Cavoodle weighs 2–3 kg and can squeeze through a gap roughly the width of his head. Treat any gap wider than about 5 cm (under the glass, between balusters, beside planters) as passable. Two layers protect him: a fortified balcony, and a toilet that means he never needs the balcony at all.

Tier 1 · Indoor toilet zone. An X-pen in the laundry or bathroom with his bed at one end and a real-grass tray at the other (Dr Ian Dunbar's "long-term confinement area"). Real grass, not fabric pads, so he learns "grass = toilet" and never generalises to rugs or bath mats.
Tier 2 · Balcony fortification. UV-stabilised HDPE balcony mesh or clear acrylic panels zip-tied taut along the lower balustrade, plus a household rule: balcony door open = Oreo on a lead in someone's hand.

Strata notes for your building

  • NSW strata by-laws can't unreasonably ban a pet, but most schemes ask owners to notify the owners corporation in writing. Check your building's pet by-law for the exact wording.
  • Balcony balustrades are usually common property. Removable, zip-tied mesh is normally fine, but a quick email to the building manager or committee (you're on it) avoids a later dispute about appearance from the street.
  • Ask the building manager when the pest contractor lays rat bait or snail pellets in common areas and where. Both are serious poisons for puppies.
  • A friendly note under neighbours' doors before 10 Nov ("new puppy, first few nights may be noisy") buys a lot of goodwill.

Daily schedule

Rule of thumb at 8–12 weeks: about 1 hour awake, then about 2 hours asleep. Over-tired puppies bite harder and have more accidents. Naps happen in the pen or crate, not on laps.

Indoor & balcony potty blueprint

  1. Take him to the grass tray on waking, within 10 minutes of eating or drinking, after play, and every 30–60 minutes he's awake.
  2. Carry him for the first weeks (puppies wee on the way). Set him on the grass, say your cue once ("go toilet"), then stand still and boring.
  3. Reward within 2 seconds of finishing, on the grass: quiet praise and a tiny treat. That timing is the whole lesson.
  4. Nothing in 2 minutes? Back into the pen or crate for 10 minutes, then try again. That's Dunbar's errorless method: he never gets free time with a full bladder.
  5. Accidents: no scolding, no nose-rubbing. Clean with an enzyme cleaner (ammonia-based cleaners smell like urine to him). Shorten the gaps between trips.
  6. Grass tray care: scoop solids at once, rinse daily, replace the turf every 1–3 weeks. Keep two trays and rotate.
  7. Balcony: not a toilet while there are gaps. After fortification you can move a second tray out there, but every balcony trip is on lead.
  8. After vaccines (see Vet tab), start pairing the indoor tray with a ground-level grass spot near your building so he can toilet on walks too.

Why the Mid-January Cliff matters

From November to mid-January three adults are home almost all day, so Oreo will learn that a person is always available. Then Mum leaves when he's about 4 months old, right as the socialisation window closes and the household routine changes. Puppies that never practised being alone are the classic setup for separation distress: howling that bothers neighbours in a strata building, chewing at the door, and toileting when left.

The fix is boring and daily: practise being alone while everyone is still home, so being alone is normal long before it becomes necessary. Emily Larlham (Kikopup) and the AVSAB both stress staying under his threshold: short, successful absences beat long, stressful ones.

The rota rule: spread feeding, toileting and play across all three adults, with you (the long-term carer) doing nights and mornings. Mum should be loved, not the only one who meets his needs.

Mid-January separation-anxiety prevention protocol

Five phases. The phase that matches today's date is highlighted. Tick each step when it's become routine.

Home-alone practice log

Log every rep. Increase the time only after two calm reps in a row at the current length; after any distressed rep, halve it.

All force-free (positive reinforcement). No yelling, no alpha rolls, no spray bottles. They damage trust and make biting and toileting problems worse.

Bite inhibition · Dr Ian Dunbar

Mouthing is how puppies learn how hard their jaws are. The goal isn't "never bite" yet; it's "always bite softly", learned before about 4½ months while his teeth are tiny.

  1. Let him mouth during calm play so he gets feedback.
  2. A hard nip ends the game: a short "ouch", then a reverse time-out. You leave (step over the gate) for 10–30 seconds instead of dragging him anywhere.
  3. Lower the bar each week: first only the hardest bites end play, then medium ones, until only gentle mouthing is tolerated.
  4. Then teach "Off" and "Take it" so mouth contact happens only on invitation.
  5. Zoomies + biting at dusk usually means over-tired. Pen with a chew and nap, not more play.

Crate Games & It's Yer Choice · Susan Garrett

  • Crate as a game: toss treats in, let him come out, reward going back in by choice. Add the door closing for a second, then longer. Use a release word ("free!") so you decide when the door opens, never his whining.
  • It's Yer Choice (impulse control): hold treats in a closed fist. When he stops pawing and licking and backs off, open your hand. If he dives, close it. When he waits, give him a treat from that hand. Progress to an open palm, then treats on the floor.
  • Why it matters here: a dog that waits by choice is a dog that won't bolt through the balcony or front door.

Garrett's full Crate Games programme is a paid course; these are the core ideas.

Capturing Calmness & cooperative care · Emily Larlham (Kikopup)

  • Capturing Calmness: keep treats nearby. Whenever Oreo lies down, sighs, or settles on his own, quietly place a treat between his paws. No cue, no excitement. Within weeks, "settle" becomes his default.
  • Mat settle: reward lying on a specific mat; this becomes his "work-from-home station" during your Teams calls.
  • Chin rest for grooming: teach him to rest his chin on your palm or a cushion. While the chin is down, you brush; if he lifts it, you pause. He gets a say, and grooming stops being a wrestle.
  • Separation: Kikopup's approach is many tiny, successful departures. That's exactly the Mid-January protocol.

Learn to Earn · Dr Sophia Yin

  • "Say please by sitting": sitting earns everything: food bowl down, lead clipped on, door opening, play starting, being picked up.
  • Every adult uses the same rules, especially important with three of you and variable shift hours.
  • Use his daily kibble as rewards in short sessions (3–5 minutes, several times a day).
  • Watch his body language during socialisation: lip-licking, yawning, whale-eye, tucked tail or freezing mean "too much". Add distance and end on an easy win.

Waitara socialisation matrix (before vaccines)

The socialisation window starts closing at about 12–16 weeks, before his vaccines are finished. So he meets the world carried, in a sling or pram, or from the car, paws off public ground. Each dot is one calm, positive exposure (at a distance where he can still eat a treat). Aim for 3+.

Summer special NYE fireworks and Sydney storms arrive at 15–16 weeks: play recordings at low volume during meals from week 1.

Line-brushing a curly Poodle-dominant coat

Surface brushing leaves mats at the skin that only shaving can remove. Line brushing works the coat in thin layers, all the way down to the skin. Daily 3–5 minutes as a puppy, with a lick mat for distraction.

  1. Kit: soft slicker brush, metal "greyhound" comb (wide + fine teeth), detangling spray, lick mat.
  2. Start at a back paw. With your free hand push the hair upward to make a horizontal parting where you can see skin.
  3. Brush the hair below the line with light, short strokes outward from the skin. Never press: slicker pins scratch ("brush burn").
  4. Comb test: slide the metal comb through to the skin. A snag = a tangle. Hold the hair at its base and tease it apart with fingers and spray.
  5. Move the line up 1 cm and repeat. Leg by leg, then the body in rows.
  • Mat hot spots: behind and under the ears, armpits, collar and harness lines, belly, inner thighs, tail and beard.
  • Never bathe a matted coat: water tightens mats. Brush out first, then dry fully while combing.
  • Ears: Cavoodles are prone to ear infections. Check weekly, dry after baths, and use only a vet-recommended cleaner.
  • First groom: a short puppy "intro" visit after his vaccines, then a full groom every 6–8 weeks.
  • Tick season trim: ask for a shorter teddy clip from spring to autumn. A short coat makes the nightly tick search far easier.

Kong recipes for puppies

Always measure from the daily ration. Freeze a batch of 4 every night: they power the home-alone reps.

  1. Week-1 easy mode: loosely drop dry kibble and a few soft treats into a puppy Kong. No freezing; he should win quickly.
  2. Soaked breakfast Kong: soak a meal's kibble in warm water for 15 minutes, mash, pack in, freeze overnight.
  3. Pumpkin layer: kibble mixed with 1 tsp plain cooked pumpkin, a whole kibble as a "cork" in the small hole, freeze.
  4. Yoghurt seal: kibble packed in, top sealed with 1 tsp plain Greek yoghurt (unsweetened, xylitol-free), freeze.
  5. Lick mat for grooming: thin smear of plain pumpkin or yoghurt, frozen flat for 20 minutes.

Paralysis tick (Ixodes holocyclus): daily search, every night

Waitara backs onto Hornsby's bushland corridors and the breeder's place is in Hunter tick country too. Paralysis ticks peak from spring through summer, exactly when Oreo arrives. Prevention chews reduce risk but never replace a nightly fingertip search, and the curly coat hides ticks well.

  1. Use your fingertips, slowly, feeling for a small bump against the skin. Start at the nose.
  2. Head and face: lips, around the eyes, chin and beard, inside and behind both ears.
  3. Neck and chest: under the collar or harness, throat, front of chest.
  4. Legs and feet: armpits, down each leg, between every toe and pad.
  5. Body: back, sides, belly, groin, under and around the tail and anus.
  6. Found one? Remove it at once with a tick hook (twist and lift) without squeezing the body, keep it in a sealed bag, and call your vet. Signs can worsen for 24–48 h after removal.
Emergency signs (go now, day or night):
  • Change in bark or voice
  • Wobbly or weak back legs
  • Vomiting or regurgitating froth
  • Coughing, gagging, noisy or grunting breathing
  • Drooling or trouble swallowing

Keep him calm and cool, and give no food or water (swallowing may be impaired).

Vaccines (AVA-aligned for Sydney)

"Due window" is calculated from Oreo's birthday. Enter the real date when each is done. Sydney and the Hunter have both had fatal canine leptospirosis cases since 2017, so lepto is part of the core plan: two doses 2–4 weeks apart, then yearly.

Parks rule: 14 days after the final C5 before ground walks, Asquith Park off-leash, or other dogs' haunts. Note: Waitara Park / Mark Taylor Oval isn't a council off-leash area; the nearest fenced off-leash park is Asquith Park (Rotherwood Ave), and Hunt Reserve in Mount Colah is another. Puppy preschool at a vet clinic is usually allowed from a week after the first vet vaccine, on cleaned indoor floors.

Intestinal worming

Every 2 weeks until 12 weeks, monthly until 6 months, then every 3 months. Use an all-wormer with praziquantel (e.g. Milbemax, Drontal): it's the ingredient that covers tapeworms, including hydatid tapeworm. Monthly tick chews don't.

Monthly tick, flea & heartworm chew

Simparica Trio from 8 weeks and ≥1.25 kg; NexGard Spectra from 8 weeks and ≥2 kg. Ask the breeder what Oreo has already had, and dose by his weight on the day (the weight log below helps). Confirm the combination with your vet.

NSW registration & admin

  • Microchip transfer: The breeder must microchip Oreo before sale and transfer ownership to you on the NSW Pet Registry. Make sure your Service NSW login works before delivery day.
  • Lifetime registration: since July 2024, NSW dogs must be registered by 12 weeks or when ownership changes, whichever comes first. For Oreo that's when ownership transfers on delivery. The 2026/27 fee is $84; pay within 28 days to avoid a $25 late fee.
  • Desexing: an extra $198 applies if he isn't desexed by 6 months. If your vet recommends waiting (common for growth in small breeds), get their written note to the registry before he turns 6 months.
  • Pet insurance: take it out before the first vet visit if you want it; conditions noted at that visit can become pre-existing exclusions, and most policies have illness waiting periods.
